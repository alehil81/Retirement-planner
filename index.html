Assumptions & methodologyDetails

Accumulation and retirement projections are deterministic, not Monte Carlo. The selected real return is applied every year, so sequence-of-returns risk is not modeled. Roth IRA contributions are entered in today's dollars through retirement; the Roth IRA is then left invested with no retirement withdrawals in this model.

Contribution escalation is applied separately to VOO and 401(k). Each phase begins at its entered base contribution and escalates annually thereafter.

During retirement, the selected percentage of VOO dividends is available for spending and the remainder is reinvested. Because the real-return assumption is a total return, VOO ending balance is modeled as beginning balance × (1 + real total return) minus only the dividends actually used for spending. All dividends remain taxable whether used or reinvested. 401(k) ending balance is beginning balance × (1 + real return) minus withdrawals.

Need-based 401(k) withdrawals are calculated to fill the estimated after-tax spending gap, subject to the available 401(k) balance. Optional moonlighting income is included through the selected age and reduces the required 401(k) draw during those years.

FERS estimate uses high-3 × service years × 1.0%, or 1.1% at age 62+ with at least 20 years of service. If full FERS survivor election is on, the retiree annuity is reduced by 10%; the survivor benefit is 50% of the unreduced annuity. Detailed COLA rules are not modeled.

Social Security uses 2026 maximum-benefit planning benchmarks: $2,969/month at 62 and $5,181/month at 70 per person. Actual future benefits may differ.

Taxes are only an approximation, not a tax return calculation. RMD rules, Medicare IRMAA, capital-gain realization from VOO share sales, state taxes, and detailed Social Security taxation are not modeled.
